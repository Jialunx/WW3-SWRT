The WRT table and SWRT, equation by equation

This page animates sections 2 and 3 of the paper “A New Nonlinear Four-Wave Interaction Scheme for Ocean Wave Models: Webb–Resio–Tracy Accuracy near DIA Cost”. Each animation follows one equation of the paper, and the terms of the equation light up as the animation reaches them.

Loci, grid points, bin areas and group velocities come from the deep-water dispersion relation on the spectral grid of the paper with 40 frequencies and 24 directions. The panels of section 3 run the least-squares fit and the greedy search on a small toy table. Each animation plays while it is on screen, and the slider under a figure moves through it by hand.

2a. The collision integral in Webb’s form

Eqs. (1)–(4)
Step 1

Four waves in resonance

𝐤1+𝐤2=𝐤3+𝐤4,ω1+ω2=ω3+ω4
(1)

Two pairs of waves (k1, k2) and (k3, k4) exchange action when both pairs have the same vector sum and the same frequency sum, with ω2 = gk in deep water. For fixed k1 and k3 the wavenumbers k2 that meet both conditions form a closed curve called the k2 locus, and the matching k4 = k1 + k2 − k3 trace the dashed k4 locus.

In the animation k2 runs once around its locus while the inset places the four vectors head to tail and the bars compare the two frequency sums. When k2 leaves the locus the vector sum still closes but the frequency sums no longer match.

Step 2

The delta functions leave one locus per pair

∂n1∂t=∭Gδ(𝐤1+𝐤2−𝐤3−𝐤4)δ(ω1+ω2−ω3−ω4)N1234d𝐤2d𝐤3d𝐤4
(2)
N1234=n1n3(n4−n2)+n2n4(n3−n1)
(3)
∂n1∂t=∫T(𝐤1,𝐤3)d𝐤3
Webb

The first delta function fixes k4 = k1 + k2 − k3, and the second confines k2 to the locus of each pair (k1, k3). The remaining integral over k3 is written as an integral of T(k1, k3) following Webb (1978), and the product term N1234 of Eq. (3) is evaluated along each locus from the action densities of the four waves.

Xnl evaluates only the pairs inside the pair window with k3/k1 ≤ 2.5 and |θ3 − θ1| ≤ 75°, which are the 54 offsets with 0 ≤ Δi ≤ 4 and |Δj| ≤ 5 other than (0, 0). The animation steps k3 through these offsets and leaves the loci of the visited pairs in gray.

Step 3

A line integral with a Jacobian and a step function

T(𝐤1,𝐤3)=2∮GJHN1234ds,J=|𝐜g,2−𝐜g,4|−1
(4)

Integration over the delta functions leaves a line integral along the k2 locus with arc length s. The gradient of the frequency mismatch Δω = ω1 + ω2 − ω3 − ω4 with respect to k2 is cg,2 − cg,4, drawn as the gray arrow at the moving point, and the Jacobian J is the inverse of its length. The gray band of small |Δω| is wider where J is larger.

The two thin lines from the tip of k1 compare |k1 − k4| with |k1 − k3|, and the step function H = 1 keeps the point where the first is longer. Each quadruplet contributes twice to the integral over k3 with the same value, in T(k1, k3) and in T(k1, k4) with k3 and k4 exchanged. The step function keeps the term with k3 closer to k1 and the factor 2 adds the equal contribution of the other.

2b. Discretization

Eqs. (5)–(6), the WRT code Xnl used by WAVEWATCH III
Step 4

The spectral grid

fi=fmin1.1i−1,i=1,…,40,Δθ=15°

The grid has 40 frequencies from fmin = 0.03453 Hz up to 1.42 Hz and 24 directions 15° apart. In deep water the wavenumbers ki are then geometrically spaced with ratio 1.12 = 1.21, and the ladder marks bin 20 at 0.21 Hz where k1 sits in the figures of this page.

Step 5

Points on the locus and their weights

T(𝐤1,𝐤3)≈2∑mwmN1234(m),wm=GmJmΔsm
(5)

Xnl places points equidistant in s on each locus, and the sum runs over the points with H = 1. Each point is one quadruplet with one weight wm that combines the coupling coefficient, the Jacobian and the step length.

At each point the thin lines connect k2 and k4 to the four surrounding grid points that give n2 and n4 by bilinear interpolation, and N1234(m) follows from Eq. (3). The bars below the figure give the four action densities and N1234(m) for a JONSWAP spectrum with its peak at 0.2 Hz.

Locus points with k2 or k4 above the highest grid wavenumber take n2 and n4 from the highest bin, and their weight includes a tail factor that corresponds to a spectral tail E(f) ∝ f−5.

Step 6

One evaluation, two bins

n˙(i1,j1)+=Tk3Δk3Δθ,n˙(i3,j3)−=Tk1Δk1Δθ
(6)

The antisymmetry T(k1, k3) = −T(k3, k1) lets a pair with i3 > i1 be evaluated once and deposited in two bins. The bin of k1 gains T times the area A3 = k3Δk3Δθ of the bin of k3, and the bin of k3 loses T times the area A1 of the bin of k1.

Each change of density times the area of its own bin is the same amount of action, and the two amounts cancel in the total. Pairs on the same frequency are evaluated once with each of their two wavenumbers as k1 and with half the step length on their straight loci. The rate of change ṅ finally becomes the source term Snl(f, θ) through E = 2πωk n/cg.

2c. Table of configurations

rows, configurations and the stencil
Step 7

The same positions at every frequency

𝐤→1.12𝐤,θ→θ+15°Δj→−Δj

In deep water multiplying k1 and k3 by a common factor scales their loci by the same factor (Tracy and Resio 1982). One frequency bin up multiplies every wavenumber of the figure and the spacing of the grid circles by 1.12, and one direction bin turns the whole figure by 15°.

Counted in bins from k1 the quadruplet keeps its positions, and only its weight wr(i1) changes with the frequency bin i1 of k1. This row of configuration 316 has k3 at the offset (2, 2) and k2 and k4 at (4.0, 7.0) and (2.4, 8.1) bins from k1.

Reflecting the directions about k1 gives the mirror image of a row, and a row and its mirror image form one configuration.

Step 8

From points to configurations

Each stored point inside the pair window gives one row, and the rows repeat at every frequency bin. The 22,914 points reduce to 1,184 rows on 54 offsets, and with their mirror images these rows form the 592 configurations of the table that Fig. 2h of the paper draws.

Step 9

A row is a stencil

n2=n(i1+Δi2,j1+Δj2),n4=n(i1+Δi4,j1+Δj4)

At each placement the row puts k1 in the anchor bin (i1, j1) and k3 in the bin (i1 + Δi, j1 + Δj), where n1 and n3 are read. The densities n2 and n4 are interpolated in the dashed boxes, and the term 2wr(i1)N1234 goes into the anchor bin and the bin of k3 by Eq. (6).

The stencil visits every bin of the grid as the anchor bin, and the colors add up the deposits of this one row with a unit weight for the JONSWAP spectrum of Fig. 4 of the paper. Summed over all anchor bins and all rows, the table reproduces WRT.

3a. From the table to SWRT

Eqs. (7)–(8)
Step 10

Two deposits per bin and row

Φq(i,j)=Ci∑r∈q[2wr(i)Nr(i,j)Ai+Δi−2wr(i−Δi)Nr(i−Δi,j−Δj)Ai−Δi]
(7)

The response Φq of configuration q is the Snl that the table gives with only the two rows of q. Bin (i, j) receives two deposits from each row, one from the evaluation anchored at (i, j) and one from the evaluation anchored at (i − Δi, j − Δj) with its k3 in bin (i, j).

The second deposit has the opposite sign and uses the weight and the area of its own anchor bin. The deposits of the row and its mirror image add up, and the factor Ci = 2πωiki/cg,i turns the rate of change of the action density into Snl.

Step 11

SWRT is a weighted sum of responses

Snl,K(E)=∑q∈𝒬KgqΦq(E)
(8)

SWRT retains K configurations of the table and multiplies the response of each by a coefficient gq. The responses are cubic in the action density through N1234, whereas Snl,K is linear in the coefficients. For given configurations the coefficients therefore follow from linear least squares.

The animation uses a toy table of 30 configurations built from real loci, with the weights JΔs in place of the coupling coefficient. It adds the first four configurations of the greedy search on six toy spectra with their refitted coefficients and compares the sum with the output of the toy table for the JONSWAP spectrum of Fig. 4.

Each spectrum enters the fit through the normalized output Ŝnl,tab of unit length, and e2 is the squared distance between the fitted sum of normalized responses and Ŝnl,tab. The overlaps of the responses with each other form M, and their overlaps with Ŝnl,tab form b.

The coefficients g = M−1b make the fitted sum the orthogonal projection of Ŝnl,tab onto the span of the retained responses. The squared residual of this projection is 1 − bTM−1b, which gives Eq. (11) after averaging over the P spectra.

The figure uses three dimensions in place of the 40 × 24 bins and a single spectrum in place of the 15,000 synthetic spectra of the paper.

Step 13

Greedy selection

qK=arg minq∉𝒬K−1e¯2(𝒬K−1∪{q})
(12)

The search starts from the empty set and adds one configuration at a time. Each candidate is tried together with the configurations already retained and with refitted coefficients, and Eq. (11) gives its error from M and b without recomputing any response.

The bars give ē2 of every candidate of the toy table at each step, and the curve traces the error of the retained set. In the paper ē2 along the greedy path falls from 0.6 with one configuration to 0.06 with 10 and 0.01 with 20, and swaps and random restarts lead to the 36 configurations of SWRT with ē2 = 0.003.

The geometry on this page follows the paper exactly, including the loci, the grid points, the bin areas and the group velocities. The coupling coefficient G is not evaluated here, and the panels that need weights use JΔs or a unit weight in its place.

The toy table of section 3 shows how the fit and the search work, and its numbers are not results of the paper.
